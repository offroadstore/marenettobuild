name: Marenetto Build publishing rules
description: Durable business constraints for claims, international content and launch readiness.
---

Marenetto Build must not publish invented projects, certifications, guarantees, staffing, equipment, prices, availability, reviews or performance claims. Technical and contractual statements require pertinent review. The site must distinguish current capabilities from planned features.

All requested language versions must cover the complete public site before being called complete. Empty routes, homepage-only translations and automatic publication of unreviewed technical, insurance or contractual text are not acceptable. A translated interface does not imply that staff speak every published language.

The owner authorizes documentary and linguistic checks assisted by AI when
external review documents are unavailable. Record actual findings, corrections
and limits, not a professional opinion or native-speaker certification.

**Why:** The owner explicitly chose to continue AI-assisted checks rather than
wait for external reviews, while keeping professional-only questions open.

**How to apply:** Keep AI review evidence separate from publication approval
and technical/legal sign-off; do not demand external attachments before doing
the documentary checks that can genuinely be completed.
Review suggestions must not erase useful explanations of Italian institutions
for foreign readers or replace native text with Italian. Automated editorial
review can recommend both mistakes; retain the localization requirements
when adjudicating its findings.

The owner accepts the regional L.R. 16/2016 consolidation incorporating
L.R. 27/2024, in force on 28 February 2025, as the dated documentary basis.
Unverified later amendments must remain in the localized “check with the
appointed professional” notice and report, not block readiness.

**Why:** The owner explicitly instructed delivery to proceed with this dated
source rather than waiting for an exhaustive later consolidation.

**How to apply:** Cite the actual validity date, verify which official URL
serves the PDF, and never describe the dated text as a November 2026 legal
certification. Record inaccessible aliases without pretending they worked.

Verified pages may be prepared as indexable without a further owner approval
round once SEO, AI transparency and actual language content have passed review.
The owner, not the agent, performs the publication.

**Why:** The owner explicitly superseded the earlier requirement for another
approval before releasing verified pages, while retaining quality checks and
personal control over Republish.

**How to apply:** Do not request the same release approval again for pages that
meet those checks. Do not treat this authorization as verification of unfinished
translations or permission to index preview/private routes or to deploy.

Preview environments must remain excluded from indexing and use synthetic data until hosting region, privacy suppliers and release prerequisites are verified.

Historical site images may be reused as illustrative material, but must not imply verified completed work, company personnel or company-owned vehicles/equipment. Prior use on the old site is not proof of licensing or releases; confirm those before public release.

**Why:** The user explicitly defined accuracy, translation quality and staged-release constraints as central product requirements, and identified themselves as the translation approver.

**How to apply:** Apply these rules to content migration, portfolio work, localization, CMS workflows, owner-area messaging and every production release.

Do not restore copied historical testimonials or name-only reviewer lists.
The owner now authorizes real reviews through official Google Places API (New),
fetched server-side with unmodified original text and required author/Google
attributions. Keep the outbound Google link as a fallback, not a verified listing.

**Why:** After removing the broken historical lists, the owner explicitly
superseded the link-only direction with an official API-backed reviews request.

**How to apply:** Apply across home and review pages in every public language;
do not generate Review/AggregateRating or infer permission for scraping, rewriting,
or unrestricted storage. Keep browser-to-Google requests out of the intended
server-only design, including author photos; the owner supplies the credentials.

Historical approval of a translation catalog must not be extended to a newer page
composition. In particular, a key can exist while still containing the English
source. Mechanical script conversion does not turn English into a translation.

Multilingual indexing must represent the language of the actual page body, not
just the navigation. Keep foreign-language wrappers around Italian content
reachable, but do not advertise them as independent translations in sitemap or
hreflang.

**Why:** The owner requested Google indexing without duplicate pages; the existing
multilingual inventory also contained Italian content behind foreign locale URLs.
Changing only titles or language labels would falsely imply translated content.

**How to apply:** Reassess eligibility when real body translations are delivered
and reviewed. Do not restore indexing merely because a locale's interface is public.

**Why:** The SEO investigation found populated review catalogs whose new commercial
phrases were unchanged English, despite a global approval flag on older locale data.

**How to apply:** Compare rendered source and translation, retain page-level approval
evidence, and keep draft corrections isolated from ordinary approved catalogs.

Preserve the thin lines of the current historical monogram and MARENETTO lettering, combined with the BUILD.COM lettering treatment from the newer banner. Do not replace the thin monogram with the banner's heavier symbol.

**Why:** After comparing both assets, the user selected MARENETTO / BUILD.COM and clarified: “preferisco le linee sottili del logo attuale con la scritta build come quella del logo nuovo”.

**How to apply:** Keep the exact logo already online (thin historical mark and blue BUILD.COM band), including its header/footer treatment and favicon. The owner explicitly prohibited changing it after a release branch introduced a different mark. General approval to merge or publish does not authorize a logo redesign.

For the production launch, use the recorded owner authorization in the production-readiness documentation instead of the historical NO-GO decision.

**Why:** The owner explicitly superseded those decision blocks while retaining technical release gates and limiting the approved language set and analytics collection.

**How to apply:** Do not ask again for the same approval; check the written scope,
keep preview and private routes unindexed, and apply the current verified-page
authorization above instead of requiring another approval for every new edition.

Open indexing only after the canonical WWW domain and permanent redirects have been verified in production.

**Why:** The owner explicitly ordered the launch this way; a valid certificate alone does not prove the redirect chain is correct. Record edge-managed HTTP redirects separately from application redirects.

2026-11-26 for the six guides plus eight specified commercial articles, in thirteen

Do not wait for project photographs: use visibly labelled illustrations without

**Why:** A successful HTTP check of the permanent verification file cannot prove

**How to apply:** Do not keep asking for the same confirmation or modify the

For the informational editorial guides and professional-network copy, the
owner explicitly accepts AI-assisted documentary and linguistic review plus
internal owner approval, recorded as such and not as a professional opinion.
Do not keep blocking editorial preparation on external professional reviewers.
Case-specific reserved technical/legal checks remain visibly open, with the

**Why:** On 2026-11-25 the owner expressly replaced the previous mandatory

**How to apply:** Bind review evidence to the actual texts, preserve source
